# Rookie Quest — Business & Website Roadmap

## Core rule
Rookie Quest is the parent brand. Build one useful product at a time, and do not advertise unfinished products as finished.

Public product order:

1. Rookie Quest Keeper — **Run** — live software product
2. Rookie Quest Forge — **Build** — next commercial product line
3. Rookie Quest Worlds — **Explore** — later content line
4. Rookie Quest RPG — **Play** — future original tabletop system

The commercial milestone is not “finish Rookie Quest.” It is:

> A new visitor can understand Rookie Quest, try something useful for free, confidently buy a real product, receive it automatically, and know where to return for updates.

---

## Phase 1 — Make Rookie Quest a credible business homepage

### Website jobs
The main site must:
- explain what Rookie Quest is in seconds;
- show which products exist and which are still planned;
- send people into Keeper;
- introduce Forge as the next product without pretending it is already for sale;
- give each product a distinct but related identity;
- work cleanly on phone, tablet and desktop;
- leave obvious future homes for Shop, Free Resources, Support and Legal.

### Exit condition
Someone unfamiliar with the project can visit the homepage and correctly explain:
- what Rookie Quest is;
- what Keeper is;
- what Forge will be;
- what is available now;
- what is coming later.

## Phase 2 — Make Forge physically real

Do not open a paid Forge catalogue until the core system is physically tested.

### Core engineering
- connector calibration and final clearance;
- universal frame connection behaviour;
- 1×1, 2×2 and 3×3 frame testing;
- floor tile fit;
- wall / corner / door interfaces;
- stairs and elevation system;
- pillars / railings / ledges;
- stacking and storage behaviour;
- print orientation and support requirements;
- real printer compatibility data.

### First release family
- **Forge Free Sample Pack** — free forever;
- **Forge Starter Set** — first paid product;
- small themed expansions after the Starter Set is stable.

### Personal-use licence direction
Normal purchases should allow:
- unlimited personal printing;
- resizing, slicing, splitting and personal modifications;
- use at home tables, clubs and personal games.

Normal purchases should not allow:
- redistribution of original or modified digital files;
- upload to other sites or file-sharing services;
- resale of STL / 3MF source files;
- commercial sale of physical prints.

A separate commercial / merchant licence can be developed later if demand exists.

### File delivery direction
Prefer:
- STL as the main printable format;
- optional 3MF where recommended orientation/settings add value;
- PDF quick-start / assembly guide;
- licence file;
- version / changelog information.

Do not use printer-specific G-code as the standard customer product format.

## Phase 3 — Build Rookie Quest Commerce v1

Only after a real Forge product exists.

### Store requirements
- product catalogue;
- product detail pages;
- free vs paid product states;
- printer/build-volume compatibility information;
- product version numbers;
- licence summary;
- checkout;
- post-purchase download access;
- purchase history / customer library;
- support/contact path;
- privacy, terms, digital-download and licensing pages.

### Payment direction
Start with the simplest reliable hosted checkout architecture. Avoid building custom card-entry UI unless there is a strong reason.

### Free funnel
Visitor flow:

1. Discover Rookie Quest
2. See Forge
3. Download the free sample
4. Print and test the system
5. Return for the Starter Set
6. Buy themed expansions over time

---

## Phase 4 — First paid launch

### Positioning
Forge should compete on:
- modularity;
- usability on normal home printers;
- fair pricing;
- clear instructions;
- attractive but practical detail;
- buy-once personal printing.

### Initial pricing hypothesis
Pricing is for testing, not a permanent promise.

- Free Sample Pack — **Free**
- Tiny accessory pack — around **£1.99**
- Small themed pack — around **£2.99**
- Standard expansion — around **£3.99–£4.99**
- Larger expansion — around **£5.99–£6.99**
- Forge Starter Set — target around **£7.99–£9.99**
- Large bundle — around **£9.99–£12.99**
- Multi-pack bundle later — around **£14.99–£19.99**

Test real customer behaviour before optimising prices.

### First revenue targets
1. First £1 from a stranger
2. First 10 paying customers
3. First £100 revenue
4. First 100 free-sample downloads
5. First repeat customer
6. First £1,000 gross trading income

---

## Phase 5 — Grow only from evidence

After Forge sales and customer behaviour exist:
- add themed Forge packs;
- improve printer filtering;
- add customer update/version notifications;
- develop merchant licensing if requested;
- release the first real Rookie Quest Worlds product;
- connect Worlds content to Keeper and Forge where useful;
- investigate paid Keeper features only after users show what advanced value they actually want;
- keep the Rookie Quest RPG last until the ecosystem has generated enough play experience to justify it.

## Product identity guide

### Rookie Quest
Midnight navy, antique gold, compass/navigation identity. Premium but welcoming parent brand.

### Keeper — Run
Warm white, deep navy and sky blue. Clean, useful, organised and approachable.

### Forge — Build
Charcoal, dark steel, warm stone and ember orange. Bold, physical, rugged and print-friendly. Branding may appear subtly on tile undersides, frame edges and maker’s marks.

### Worlds — Explore
Atlas / campaign-tome feeling. Parchment, gold and restrained natural colours.

### Rookie Quest RPG — Play
Future identity. Dramatic, rules-book worthy and clearly related to the parent brand, but not designed in detail until the game itself is real.

---

## Public truthfulness rule
It is fine to show the roadmap.

Do not show:
- fake stock;
- fake customer counts;
- fake reviews;
- fake checkout buttons;
- product photos that imply an unfinished prototype is a finished commercial item;
- paid packs that do not exist yet.

Rookie Quest should look ambitious without asking customers to guess what is real.
